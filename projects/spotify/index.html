My listening, in data

Years of my Spotify streaming history: what, when and how I listen.

Data
My Spotify extended streaming history (personal data export)

Before the data

Why this page? It is not an advert for Spotify. A streaming app happens to log every play, and that log sits where two of my interests meet: music and data analysis. So I asked for my history and read it the way I read data at work.

I came to Spotify late, and slowly. I don’t remember when I signed up. The first play in my export is from 2016, but I was on the free plan and hardly used it: the early years are thin, with whole months empty. My music lived elsewhere, in my own MP3 library played with VLC, on MP3 players, and now and then on YouTube.

Around 2021 I moved to the paid plan, and Spotify became the place where I listen. So this is not ten years of my listening. It is the story of how one app took over from the others, and of what I listened to once it had.

What the data says, so far

Two guesses I had before looking:

  1. I explore more since Spotify became my main player. The data agrees, and not only because I listen more. Drawing the same number of plays (150) from each year, the free-plan years give 7 to 34 different artists, the paid years 65 to 93. Genres and themes need more data: that is a later step.
  2. Podcasts have a turning point. Two, in fact: I started in March 2021, at around 10 hours a month, and in September 2023 I jumped to about 27 hours a month.

And one I didn’t plan: the music series dates my move to the paid plan. Monthly music hours step up in May 2021, from about 4 to 15 hours a month.
