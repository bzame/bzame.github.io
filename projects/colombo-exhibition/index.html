Bernardo Colombo: an exhibition as a factor analysis

A student-made exhibition on the founder of statistics at Padua, told through five "latent factors" instead of a timeline. I was one of its web editors.

Client
University of Padua, Department of Statistical Sciences
My role
Web editor, with the other web curators

The exhibition's own website, still online at the University of Padua.

The exhibition website (Italian)

The exhibition

Bernardo Colombo founded the Faculty of Statistical Sciences at the University of Padua and worked across biometry, demography, statistics and economics. In 2014 a group of statistics students, with the Department of Statistical Sciences, put together an exhibition about him: not only the scientist, but the person behind the career.

The idea: a factor analysis of a life

Instead of a chronological path, the curators did what statisticians do: they looked for a few independent “latent factors” that explain most of Bernardo Colombo’s “variability”, and measured each one with “indicators” (episodes, documents, testimonies). Five factors came out, and the exhibition is organised around them. As the introduction admits, not all the variability was explained.

What I did

I joined when the printed exhibition was already finished, to bring it online.

  • Built the website: the pages follow the structure and content of the printed posters, and I wrote all of their code.
  • Prepared the images for the web, reducing their resolution so the pages would load fast.

It took a couple of weeks of work. What stayed with me is something else: I didn’t know who Bernardo Colombo was. Working through the exhibition’s material, I got to know him a little, and came to admire the person as much as the statistician.
